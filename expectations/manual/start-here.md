# Start here

This manual has three paths through it. Pick the one for what you want to do, and do its pages in order. Each tutorial takes a few minutes. Each path starts at [[Requirements and installation]]. The parts of the manual follow the paths: Learn to fly, Ground control and Towers.

## Learn to fly

You fly the T1-S Ranger by hand first. Then you fly the T1-S VTOL, which has a flight controller.

1. [[Requirements and installation]]: download SIM001, or build it.
2. [[Your first flight from the launcher]]: start the sim from the launcher.
3. [[Controls]]: the keys, the gamepad and the radio. Set up a radio or a gamepad now, if you have one: [[Setting up your radio]].
4. [[Your first throw]], then [[Watching from every view]], [[Crashing and starting again]] and [[Taking a break]].
5. [[Your first hover]] and [[Learning with self-levelling]]: the VTOL, and the modes that its flight controller adds. Start the sim again for these: in the launcher, click SIMULATOR ONLY, then WITH ARDUPILOT, then LAUNCH.
6. [[Flying a VTOL]]: take off, fly forward, hover and land. This tutorial uses the ground station: in the launcher, click GROUND STATION, then WITH ARDUPILOT, then LAUNCH.
7. With a TX15: [[Setting up the TX15]], then [[Taking off with the TX15]].

Then have some fun with [[Your first pylon race]] and [[A game of tag]].

## Learn ground control

The ground station flies the T1-S VTOL through ArduPilot, as Mission Planner does. You plan a mission on a map, you write it to the plane, and the plane flies it. The same console connects to a real aircraft, and its mission files open in Mission Planner.

1. [[Requirements and installation]]. Then start the ground station: in [[The launcher]], click GROUND STATION, then WITH ARDUPILOT, then LAUNCH. Wait until the ARM button of the console shows ARM, not ARM: NOT READY. This takes about a minute.
2. [[The screens of the console]]: what each screen is for.
3. [[From plan to flight]]: the whole job, from an empty map to a plane that flies a mission.
4. [[Planning a hand-launched circuit]], [[Fixing a misplaced waypoint]] and [[Clearing the ridge]]: plan a mission well.
5. [[Flying the circuit and coming home]]: fly it, and watch the plane on the map.
6. [[Keeping a mission for the field]] and [[Surveying a field]]: save a plan, and plan a survey.
7. [[The battlefield view]]: see the plane, the valley and the plan in 3D.
8. [[Connecting to a real aircraft]]: take the console and your plan to the field.

## Play Towers

Towers is a game for two teams of drones. You do not fly the drones. You command them from the commander app.

1. [[Requirements and installation]]. Then start a practice game: in [[The launcher]], click TOWERS, then RED: NOBODY, then LAUNCH. The 3D view loads the valley first, which takes some minutes. [[Starting a game of Towers]] shows the choices.
2. [[The rules of Towers]]: read how a tower changes sides and how to win. Its first page, [[Taking a tower]], is enough to start.
3. [[Your first tower]], then [[Changing your mind]].
4. [[Watch your drone on the battlefield]] and [[Circle a point, then fly a route]].
5. Play against the computer: in the launcher, click TOWERS, then RED: COMPUTER, then LAUNCH. [[Controls]] lists the keys of the commander app.
6. If you build from source: [[The game with ArduPlane drones]] plays a whole game between two computer teams.

## Desktop, iOS, sim and field

The menu at the top of each page picks the version of the manual. **Desktop** is the app on a Mac, Linux or Windows computer, and is what most readers want. **iOS** is the ground station on an iPad or iPhone. **Sim** and **field** are for Towers and the flight computer: sim is the game with simulated drones, and field is real drones and a real radio. A page that is the same everywhere looks the same in each version.
